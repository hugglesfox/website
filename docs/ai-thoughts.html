My thoughts on LLM usage

Published 2026/02/06

Heads up this one is an opinion piece.

These is my thoughts and feelings at the date of publishing. These thoughts and feelings might change and they don't have to be yours.

Lately my boss has been drinking the LLM koolaid, touting 5x, 10x or even 20x efficiency gains through using LLMs to write code. The conversations which this mentality has created has forced me to really reflect on what AI means to me.

I use LLMs almost daily myself in my home assistant setup. I have a small model running locally on a cheap, lower power GPU to do basic low consequence reasoning and summarising for automations. For an example, I usually do the washing in the morning and hang it outside on the clothesline. Then I have the issue that I then go about my day and forget about the clothes until I remember them last minute at night and hurry out to bring them in. This results in clothes being outside in the sun for 8+ hours.

So I have an LLM which digests the current weather forecast then attempt to predict how many hours it will take for the washing to dry. Then home assistant sets a timer and sends me a notification when my clothes are ready to be brought in. The following is the output of it's reasoning, as an aside it's funny how it converts the kph wind estimates to mph.

To determine the minimum hours needed to dry clothes before sunset, I need

- Current time: 2026-03-31 11⭐18 UTC+11:00

## Minimum Drying Hours Calculation:

For the minimum drying time, I'm considering optimal conditions:

- **1 hour**: May be too aggressive for most fabric types
- **1.5 hours**: Borderline for many garments
- **2 hours**: Safe for quick-dry conditions with strong wind and sunshine

However, given that you have 8 hours before sunset and the optimal weather
conditions, I recommend **6 hours** as the minimum practical time.

This recommendation accounts for:

- Conservative margin of safety
- Variation in fabric drying rates
- Potential wind variability
- Ensures complete drying to prevent mildew
- Well within the 8-hour window available

Now I could very well create an algorithm that's able to deterministically calculate the number of hours required to dry my washing for any given weather forecast, however getting a fuzzy result from an LLM is usually going to be good enough and is quicker to implement.

To me LLMs are simply a tool, and yes this tool has downsides like any other

  • Hallucinations
  • High computing cost for training and running
  • Dubious interpretations of copyright law when collecting training data
  • Questionable copyright status of generated content
  • And more

Though with time and I do believe that there are going to be at least partial solutions to most of these problems, such as freely licensed training data, distilling to make small models that can run efficiently on consumer hardware with acceptable results, etc. Therefore there might be niche use cases where LLMs might make sense.

But there is an issue which I don't believe is being talked about enough and that is the masking of individual expression.

AI Homogenization

The thing that scares me the most about the widespread adoption of LLMs to summarise, edit and create text is the way in which it likes to convert everything into a standardized phrasing and formatting. This paves over nuance and careful consideration, making everything sound like it's been written by an overenthusiastic middle level manager.

Documentation, stories, opinions, code, as soon as an LLM is introduced into the mix, even if it's just as an external aid, it influences the writer and the audience and everything starts to slowly homogenize towards the LLM's voice.

Thus none of my content on this blog, mastodon or code on Codeberg/GitHub ever has been nor is ever going to be created or edited with an LLM. With the exception of directly quoting an LLM such above.

The best defence we have right now against AI homogenization is disclosure. If you use LLMs in any capacity to create a piece, please disclose it. That way at least your audience is somewhat aware that it is the voice and reasoning of an LLM and not of a human.
